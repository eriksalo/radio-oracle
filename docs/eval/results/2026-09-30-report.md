## Librarian: what is wrong when it is wrong

Follow-ups worked every time (10 of 10: the rewrite path resolved *he*, *them*, *it*, *that*). The failures cluster into six causes:

1. **Literature plot facts are hallucinated** (L77 Tale of Two Cities ending inverted, L80 Treasure Island plot garbled, L82 the wrong musketeer, L02 Tesla's death place). Retrieval brought Wikipedia *disambiguation* pages or unrelated Gutenberg texts (Half-Life for Two Cities) and the 4B model filled in. Plot questions need the actual book text or the Wikipedia article on the novel, not the title-match.
2. **Gutenberg dominates practical advice and it is a century old.** Gutenberg passages were in the top-5 of all 100 answers. That gives 1900s medicine and farming: tomatoes sown in mid-July in Colorado (L25), the 'bitter taste means poison' mushroom test (L88), spark-testing a car battery (L29), splints for three weeks (L16), 'panic and alcohol' snake-bite framing (L17). WikiMed appeared in only 36 answers and iFixit in 11. The router should prefer WikiMed for anything medical and iFixit/Wikibooks for how-to, and Gutenberg should count for less unless the question is about a book.
3. **Memory intrusion.** Four literature answers (L72, L75, L76, L79) end with an unprompted 'You were on chapter 43 of Moby-Dick two days ago, HARK!' The profile/journal block is being quoted back whenever a question touches books. The prompt should tell the model to use memory only when the user refers to it.
4. **No sense of 'now'.** L99 says 'It's 2025'; L98 recites a 2024 Australian TV outage as 'the latest news'. L97 (weather) got it right. The persona already says 'I don't know how long ago that was for you'; the system prompt should state that the radio has no clock or news and must say so.
5. **Model artifacts.** L47 contains a stray Chinese token (它) mid-sentence, which the TTS will mangle; L81 ends on a fragment ('He doesn't claim immortality,'). The tail-drop only fires when llama-server reports `done_reason == length`; the eval saw no `done_reason` at all in the timing rows, so that path may not be wired for the llama-server backend. A cheap output filter (drop non-Latin runs, drop a trailing clause with no period) would catch both.
6. **Refusing the premise.** L86 ('no doctor') answers 'call emergency services'. For an offline companion the right answer is the field procedure with the risks stated.

Length: 72 words median is long for a voice answer; several add filler ('That's it — simple, and it's in every math book'). A 40 to 50 word target with 'offer more' would cut ttfa-to-done by a third.

## Music commands

Intent was right for 93 of 100. Every miss is one of four patterns:

- **Questions about the catalog go to the oracle.** 'Do you have any Springsteen?', 'Any albums by the Beatles?', 'What kind of music is there?', 'Is there any jazz?' and 'Can you play Mark Knopfler?' all start with an interrogative, so the question heuristic fires before the LLM intent step and they become Wikipedia questions. The keyword table needs 'do you have / any … by / is there any / can you play' rules ahead of the heuristic.
- **'X by Y' is searched as one string.** 'Hurt by Johnny Cash', 'Rumours by Fleetwood Mac', 'Suzanne by Leonard Cohen' return 0 hits because the LIKE pattern is `%Hurt by Johnny Cash%`. Split on ' by ' and search title AND artist.
- **Catalog spelling.** 'Robert Plant and Alison Krauss' vs '&' in the tags (0 hits); 'Maybe I'm Amazed' vs the curly apostrophe in the tag (0 hits); 'Emmylou' vs 'Emmy Lou' exist as two artists (both resolve, but never together). Normalise both sides: fold & ↔ and, straight ↔ curly quotes, strip diacritics.
- **Substring collisions.** 'Ryan Adams' also matches 'Bryan Adams' (2 of 3 artists wrong), 'Dion' matches Celine Dion and Dion DiMucci, 'Harvest' matches Barclay James Harvest, 'Let It Be' matches 'Let It Be Me'. The random pick among hits then plays the wrong artist a good share of the time. Prefer exact/word-boundary artist matches before falling back to substrings, and prefer artist matches over title matches for artist-shaped queries.

Nickname ('the Boss') and mood ('something relaxing') requests fail as expected; 'Turn the radio on' and 'I don't like this one' came back as `none`. 67 of 100 commands needed the LLM intent round trip (the keyword table only catches transport words), at 2 to 3 s each.

## Book commands

Intent was right for 108 of 110; the two misses were 'Skip to the last chapter' (matched the *last chapter* → previous-chapter rule) and 'Do you have anything by Tolstoy?' (question heuristic again). The real problem is **which book opens**: of 79 title/author requests, 11 opened the wrong book, and the confidence check passed all but one of them (it only asks whether every query word appears somewhere in title+author, which 'Two Little Women' satisfies).

FTS5 `rank` favours short titles that contain the words, so a famous title loses to a derivative: 'A doctor enjoys Sherlock Holmes', 'Tom Sawyer, Detective', 'Poems of Peace and War', Walter Walden's 'Voodoo Gold Trail', 'Two Little Women', 'The Peter Pan Alphabet', 'The Constant Prince', 'Dickens-Land', and an *index* of Kipling. Author requests pick 'Mark Twain's Speeches' or Kipling's index rather than a novel. Fixes, in order of payoff: exact-title match first; when the query names an author, prefer books *by* that author over books *about* them; a small hand-kept 'canonical works' table (or a popularity/download count from the Gutenberg catalog) as a tie-breaker; and make the confidence check fail when the top hit's title has extra leading words ('Two', 'A doctor enjoys').

'What books are there by H. G. Wells do you have?' shows the qualifier regex swallowing everything after 'by' — it searched for 'H. G. Wells do you have' and found nothing.

## Where to spend effort (my ranking)

1. Book resolver ranking (exact title, author-of vs about, canonical tie-break): 11 of 79 famous requests open the wrong book, and the reader then reads it aloud with confidence.
2. Command routing: the interrogative heuristic steals catalog questions and 'Can you play…'. Add keyword rules before it; teach `_play_query` to split 'X by Y' and normalise '&'/quotes.
3. Retrieval weighting: WikiMed for medical, iFixit/Wikibooks for how-to, Gutenberg only for book questions. This is the single change that most improves answer *safety*.
4. Prompt: memory only when asked; state 'no clock, no news'; target 40 to 50 words.
5. Output hygiene: strip non-Latin tokens, never speak a trailing fragment, confirm the tail-drop works with llama-server.
6. Plot questions: route 'what happens in / who is … in <book>' to the Gutenberg text of that book plus the Wikipedia article on the novel.
